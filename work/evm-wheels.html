EVM Wheels

A responsive self-drive rental platform redesigned to make search, pricing, checkout, documents, and support feel transparent for tourists, business travelers, and local riders.

6 months

Audience
Tourists, business travelers, locals, weekend riders, returning rental customers

Brief

Turn a cautious rental journey into a confident booking flow.

EVM Wheels offers self-drive car and bike rentals across Kerala. The service had to work for visitors planning a trip, locals booking a short ride, and business travelers who needed a vehicle quickly with minimal ambiguity.

The core UX problem was not lack of demand. Users wanted the vehicles, but the booking experience created uncertainty around price, availability, documents, pickup terms, and support. Each moment of doubt added friction before payment.

The redesign focused on one promise: users should know what they are booking, what it costs, and what happens next.

Problem

The rental process asked users to trust too much, too late.

Before the redesign, users struggled to compare vehicles and understand the total cost of a booking. Critical details like deposit, taxes, insurance, refund policies, pickup instructions, and support access were scattered across the journey instead of being available when decisions were being made.

Design strategy

Structure the product around control, not persuasion.

Search

Fast intent capture

Location, date, vehicle type, and pricing were placed upfront so users could start from their real trip context.

Filter

Comparison that works

Budget, brand, vehicle type, fuel, transmission, ratings, and availability made the inventory easier to narrow.

Price

No hidden math

Deposits, taxes, insurance, fees, and add-ons were separated so users could understand the total before checkout.

Support

Help stays visible

A persistent support entry point gave users reassurance during booking, pickup, return, and cancellation.

Audit to redesign

The older product had useful parts, but users had to assemble the booking logic themselves.

The older EVM Wheels website already contained the raw ingredients of a strong rental platform: vehicle cards, category tabs, filters, booking dates, countdowns, and support. The issue was hierarchy. Search context lived on the left, vehicle comparison lived in the center, support floated on the edge, and price/policy details were not connected tightly enough to the decision.

I used the audit to reorganize the product around five questions users actually asked: Is this available? What does it cost? Can I change pickup or drop-off? What documents are needed? Who helps me if something changes?

KeepVisible inventory, category tabs, vehicle photography, and quick support access.
FixScattered pricing, weak pickup context, unclear document timing, and repeated decision points.
RebuildA service journey that moves from trip intent to comparison to confirmation to post-booking control.

Yellow-theme screens are from the older website I redesigned; they are shown here as audit material and interaction evidence.

Booking start

The redesigned entry point captures trip intent before showing inventory.

The first screen was redesigned around the minimum information needed to show relevant vehicles: pickup location, pickup time, return behavior, and drop-off details. This reduces browsing noise and helps users feel that the product understands their trip before asking them to compare cars.

IntentPickup, date, and return logic are treated as the start of the booking, not a form afterthought.
TrustBrand range and vehicle quality are visible immediately, while the CTA remains focused on selection.
MomentumUsers can start broad, then narrow by category, budget, transmission, fuel, and brand in the listing flow.

Inventory and selection

Large listing screens make the comparison logic legible.

The listing experience needed to support two behaviors at once: quick selection for users who already knew what they wanted, and careful comparison for users balancing budget, comfort, vehicle type, luggage, fuel, and transmission. I separated trip context, filters, vehicle details, and selection actions so the page could scan like a booking tool rather than a crowded catalog.

Profile and service layer

The rental journey continues after the user chooses a vehicle.

Rentals create follow-up questions: document upload, booking status, support, pickup information, payment, cancellation, and repeat booking. The redesigned profile layer brings those states together so customers can manage the booking without hunting across emails, calls, or scattered pages.

Saved details Booking status Document upload Pickup guidance Live support

Screen system

The final screens connect search, listing, selection, and management into one service loop.

The final direction kept the high-utility parts of the older website but rebuilt the hierarchy around a service lifecycle: search, compare, book, manage, and get help. That made the product feel less like a catalog of cars and more like a rental system that knew what could go wrong.

Usability study

Testing validated the new flow and revealed the next layer of clarity.

I ran remote, unmoderated usability sessions with more than 20 participants aged 18-50. Each session focused on whether users could search, compare, understand total cost, complete checkout, and find help without explanation.

20+ Participants across business, leisure, tourist, and local rental contexts.

Users wanted different pickup and drop-off locations

The search module was updated to make flexible drop-off behavior clearer instead of treating it like an edge case.

Pricing transparency improved trust, but the UI needed less clutter

The cost breakdown was refined with clearer hierarchy, icons, and color-coded groups for deposits, taxes, and optional costs.

Document upload could move later

Allowing users to complete payment before uploading certain documents reduced interruption and made checkout feel lighter.

Live chat helped, but needed better visibility

Support moved into a floating, always-available entry point across booking, pickup, and return contexts.

Refinement

The final product solved for repeated confidence, not one-time conversion.

For first-time users

Clear filters, upfront pricing, pickup instructions, cancellation details, and support reduced uncertainty before payment.

For returning users

Saved preferences, quicker booking, optional later document upload, and dashboard access made repeat rentals more efficient.
